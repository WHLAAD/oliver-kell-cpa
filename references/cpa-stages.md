# CPA stage reference

Use this reference when classifying a chart. The thresholds are consistency heuristics, not official Oliver Kell rules. Structure and cycle location outweigh any isolated numerical signal.

## Shared evidence

Short-term bullish evidence includes price above EMA10, EMA10 above EMA20, and both averages rising. Intermediate confirmation includes price above a rising SMA50 and constructive pullbacks. Long-term confirmation includes price above SMA200 and SMA50 above SMA200.

Volume is constructive when it expands on reversals or breakouts and contracts on pullbacks. Repeated heavy-volume declines, weak-volume breakouts, high-volume failures, and climax volume after mature runs are warnings.

Relative strength means performance versus the selected benchmark. Compare 20-session and 63-session returns and inspect whether relative strength improves while price bases or weakens before a breakdown.

## 1. Reversal Extension

Interpretation: a prior decline is stretched and price is attempting a reversal.

Supporting evidence:

- A clear preceding decline.
- Price materially below declining or recently declining EMA10/EMA20.
- Negative extension, often near `Ext10 <= -1.5` or `Ext20 <= -2.0`.
- A meaningful prior low, weekly level, gap area, or washout support.
- A reversal candle, undercut-and-reclaim, strong close, or sharp rejection.
- Volume expansion, ideally near `RelVol >= 1.3`, or other evidence of selling exhaustion.

Potential trigger: a break above the reversal bar or nearby pivot.

Invalidation: decisive loss of the reversal low or setup support.

Do not label a falling stock Reversal Extension solely because it is oversold. Without reversal evidence, it remains weak.

## 2. Wedge Pop

Interpretation: after a reversal or early advance, price compresses and breaks upward as a new trend organizes.

Supporting evidence:

- A recent reversal or trend transition.
- Roughly 3-10 or more bars of tightening action, constructive higher lows, or a small wedge.
- Range or volume contraction.
- EMA10 flattening or turning higher.
- Price reclaiming and holding EMA10/EMA20.
- A break above the compression high with constructive volume.

Potential trigger: a break above the wedge high.

Invalidation: failure back through the wedge followed by loss of the setup low or EMA structure.

Prefer early-cycle Wedge Pops with limited overhead supply.

## 3. EMA Crossback

Interpretation: a confirmed uptrend pulls back toward rising EMA10/EMA20 and resumes. This is a high-priority CPA setup.

Supporting evidence:

- A meaningful prior thrust.
- Rising or improving EMA10 and EMA20, preferably `EMA10 > EMA20`.
- A first or early pullback toward EMA10/EMA20.
- Price within roughly 0.5 ATR of an EMA, or a brief undercut and reclaim.
- Contracting pullback volume when available.
- A bullish reversal or resumption bar.
- Constructive relative strength.

Penalties include repeated deep EMA20 tests, flat or falling EMA20, heavy-volume closes below EMA20, or obvious distribution.

Potential trigger: a break above the rebound bar or nearby pivot.

Invalidation: the setup low or a decisive loss of the EMA/support structure.

## 4. Base & Break

Interpretation: an established or emerging uptrend forms a base and breaks resistance.

Supporting evidence:

- An objective pivot or resistance level.
- A base usually lasting at least 10 trading days; shorter flags require exceptional momentum.
- Constructive consolidation, tightening ranges, higher lows, or volatility contraction.
- Supportive EMA20 and preferably SMA50.
- A breakout above the pivot.
- Breakout volume ideally near `RelVol >= 1.3`.
- Strong or improving benchmark-relative performance.

Penalties include repeated failed breakouts, wide or loose action, heavy overhead supply, extreme EMA extension, and weak relative strength.

Potential trigger: a break and hold above the base pivot.

Invalidation: a failed breakout back into the base or loss of structurally relevant support.

Distinguish an early base from a late-stage base after a mature run.

## 5. Exhaustion Extension

Interpretation: a mature uptrend has become abnormally stretched and may be climactic.

Supporting evidence:

- A mature prior advance followed by sharp acceleration.
- Extension often near `Ext10 >= +2.0` or `Ext20 >= +3.0`.
- Consecutive wide-range advances or late gaps.
- Very high relative volume, vertical action, or euphoria.
- A reversal wick or failed continuation.

Extension does not prove a top. Treat this primarily as an avoid-chasing and risk-management condition, not an automatic short signal. For existing longs, consider structure-based trimming or tighter trailing risk. For new entries, usually wait.

## 6. Wedge Drop

Interpretation: a prior uptrend is losing control and breaks short-term trend support.

Supporting evidence:

- A mature or damaged prior uptrend.
- Lower highs, a topping wedge, a failed breakout, or distribution.
- Loss of EMA10 followed by EMA20.
- EMA10 rolling over.
- A failed reclaim of the EMA zone.
- Distribution volume and weakening relative strength.

A stronger version breaks EMA20 or important support, bounces weakly, rejects at EMA10/EMA20, and resumes lower.

For existing longs, treat this as defensive or exit risk. Do not recommend a short unless the user explicitly requests bearish trading analysis.

## Transitional charts

When evidence spans stages, name one primary stage and one secondary or developing stage. Common transitions include Reversal Extension to Wedge Pop, EMA Crossback to Base & Break, and Exhaustion Extension to Wedge Drop. Reduce confidence when the transition lacks a confirmed trigger.
